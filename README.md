# Global Education & Workforce Gender Gap Analysis (1990–2022)

An exploratory data analysis and visualization project examining how gender parity in education compares to women's labour force participation across global regions from 1990 to 2022.

## Key Questions
1. How do gender gaps in education compare to gaps in labour force participation across regions?
2. Is there a relationship between achieving gender parity in education and women participating in the labour force?

## Tools & Methodology
* **Microsoft Excel:** Cleaned and standardized raw datasets (1990–2022) and built Pivot Tables to analyze regional parity patterns.
* **Tableau:** Created line charts, bar charts, and regional maps to visualize female progress in education and workforce participation over time.

## Key Insights & Conclusion
* **Education gaps are closing, but workforce gaps remain:** Across every region, gender gaps in education have decreased significantly as more women access education. However, female labour force participation has not increased at the same pace.
* **Regional highlights:**
  * **Sub-Saharan Africa:** Stands out from other regions—in several countries, women are ahead or equal in workforce participation even where they lag behind in education.
  * **Europe & Central Asia:** Leads other regions, with women's participation in both education and the workforce growing faster than anywhere else.
* **Weak link between education parity and employment parity:** Based on Pivot Table analysis, closing the education gap does not automatically translate into equal workforce participation. For example, in **East Asia & the Pacific (1990–1999)**, only **2.56%** of records showed women behind in both education and work, while **9.51% out of 13.91%** showed women had reached education parity yet still lagged behind in the workforce. Outside of Sub-Saharan Africa, this same pattern repeats across regions.
